Governance scales

How participatory, transparent and hierarchical each governance option is, from 1 to 7: the scores behind every published card's scales, and the drafts behind the suggestions (value links, size fit), here for the team to review. Not linked from the site.

How a card's scores work

  • A card's score on each scale is the average of the scores of what it chose: its decision-making approaches, how people join (tiers and ways) and its conflict management approaches, rounded to a whole number. The card says what it's based on (e.g. “Participatory 5 / 7 · based on 6 choices”).
  • — means an option says nothing about that scale; it's left out of the average (not counted as low).
  • If nothing a card chose is scored on a scale, that scale shows N/A.
  • Only accepted scores count; proposed ones are drafts waiting for review.
  • A community also says where it would like to be on each scale (Basics, 1–7: its target scales, shown publicly only in Full). Values are scored on where they point; with the community's description (each value's words and phrases, see Value groups and cues), that suggests values: the best 5 are Suggested, others that fit Also fit. The same scales help suggest approaches. Try it on test-recs.html.

Reviewing

For each option, check its three scores and its reason (the reason may be quoted to communities, so it should read well on its own). Send changes to Sohyeon, or edit data/governance_scales.json directly; once a score is agreed, its status becomes accepted. This page always shows the file as it is.

Values

Values are scored on where they point: where a community that holds the value would tend to want to be (not what the value “is”). They suggest values in Basics: a value's distance from a community's targets is the average, over the three scales, of the difference; — counts as an average fit (a distance of 2, neutralDistance in the file), so a value can't come out close just by saying less. A value's scale part goes from +1 (at the targets) through 0 (an average fit) to −1; it's added to the description's part (see Value groups and cues), and the best get chips, with this reason. Many values say nothing about how a community is run, and stay — on all three; they're still found through the list, and later through “More like this”.

Value groups and cues

Drafts to review, in data/value_groups.json (kept apart from CommunityRule's values.json, which may be refreshed from its API). Each value is in one group, shown as tabs on the values list: Goals & causes, How we organize, How we treat each other, Justice & inclusion, Practices, and Widely shared (values most good-faith communities hold). Its cues are words and phrases a community that holds it might use in its description; they're matched to suggest the value, quoting what matched. A cue matches whole words; a trailing * matches any ending (organiz*: organize, organizing); a cue of two or more words counts double. Cues should be specific: a common word (e.g. rest, fair) will match often. 18 descriptions were CommunityRule placeholders and are rewritten.

How suggestions work

In Membership and Processes, each decision-making approach, way of joining and conflict approach gets a score from three parts, each from −1 to +1:

  • Scale fit (counts in full): how close its scores above are to where the community would like to be. +1 at its targets, 0 at an average fit (a distance of 2), down to −1.
  • Value support (counts in full): the community's values' links to it, from −2 (argues strongly against) to +2 (argues strongly for), added up; ±2 = ±1.
  • Size fit (counts for half): how well it suits the community's size, from −2 to +2; ±2 = ±1.

Two tiers, with no cap: options scoring at least 1 (e.g. two good reasons, or one strong one) are Suggested; those scoring at least 0.5 (e.g. one value for it and nothing against it, or a close fit on the scales) Also fit, with a quieter chip. Each has a line for each part that applies, including what counts against it. Lists show Suggested options first, then Also fits, then the rest A–Z. The weights and cut-offs are in js/recommend.js.

More like this / Try something new: when someone selects an approach or a value, a row under their choices offers the 3 with the closest scores on the three scales, and the 2 with the most opposite scores plus 1 at random. For one with no scores (— on all three), it offers 3 at random. Clicking one adds it.

While the drafts are in review, suggestions use proposed scores, links and size fits too, so they can be tried out. Only accepted ones count after launch.
