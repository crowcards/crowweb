About

CROW Cards helps groups — from local clubs and open science projects, to online fora and team workspaces — build intentional online spaces that can learn from, connect to, and coordinate with a broader network. The cards implement a structured data schema that provides a shared language about how communities at different scales and forms govern online interaction.

A community-based collaboration between Princeton HCI and Bonfire Networks, it’s also part of a research program on how to move toward an open web that everyday people can have a say in.

We’re working on:

  • Iteratively designing the web app for generating cards
  • Collecting a dataset of cards from real-world communities
  • Co-designing mechanisms and tools for trust-based sharing, like sharing moderation logs and reports across communities

How it works

If you’re a group of any kind looking to manage (or already managing) an online space for your community, give us a spin!

CROW Cards will ask you to walk through core aspects of what your community does and how it operates. We recommend filling it out with others in your community, perhaps in a town hall. We also run virtual workshops with communities who want our support doing this.

You’ll be able to download a card that you can display on your community and the raw JSON data that contains your answers. You’ll also be able to set how much about your governance practices you want to share publicly (i.e., to the rest of the world) versus privately (i.e., to just your community).

Finally: consider contributing your CROW Card to the open library hosted on this site for others to learn from, perhaps fork from!
