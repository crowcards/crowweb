Fit

How well each value and approach suits a community's type, format and size: marks from −2 (strongly against) to +2 (strongly for), each with a reason; a blank means no opinion.

gate = generally not a fit (no chip, the reason shown); interesting = rare for this kind of group but viable (offered by “Try something new”). Typical is the option's usual mark across all community types: suggestions use the difference from it. Each type's example communities are checked against its marks. Files: data/community_types.json, data/type_value_fit.json, data/type_approach_fit.json.

Rarely a fit anywhere

For every community type: options that are a poor fit almost everywhere get no chip, with the reason shown, unless a community type marks them for or interesting (data/option_flags.json).

Values

Approaches

How well each approach suits a community's size (data/approach_size_fit.json); — means size makes no real difference. Small (under ~50), Medium (~50 to a few hundred), Large (a few hundred to a few thousand), XL very large (more).

How these were scored

Score how the approach behaves at each size as it's normally used, without assuming more people take part in each process as the community grows. Strain counts only when the approach by its nature needs the whole membership (everyone must agree; every member approves each newcomer; a community-wide vote on a dispute), needs people to know each other personally, or makes a few people's work grow with every member or case (one leader deciding everything; a few reviewers reading every application). “Suits it” means designed for many people (delegation, sampling, standing bodies). Formal structure in a small group is overhead, not failure: at most −1.

Positions on the scales

Where each option and value sits on Participatory, Transparent and Hierarchical, from 1 to 7 (data/governance_scales.json); — means it says nothing about that scale. These give every published card its scales, and feed the scales evidence of every suggestion. The reasons may be quoted to communities, so they should read well on their own.

How a card's scores work
  • A card's score on each scale is the average of the scores of what it chose (decision-making, how people join, conflict management), rounded to a whole number, with what it's based on (e.g. “Participatory 5 / 7 · based on 6 choices”).
  • An option marked — is left out of the average (not counted as low). With nothing scored on a scale, the card shows N/A.
  • A community also says where it would like to be on each scale (Basics, its target scales); suggestions compare these with the positions here.

Value word lists

Each value has a word list: words and phrases that, when a community uses them in its Purpose, Culture or keywords, suggest that value (“we look after each other” → Community care, Because you wrote “look after each other”). Matching is by word stem, so “helping each other” also matches “help each other”; a trailing * matches any ending. Also here: each value's tab on the values list, and its description (ours where CommunityRule's was a placeholder). How the lists were made: RECOMMENDATIONS.md, “The rebuild”. Files: data/value_groups.json; proposals from node scripts/make-value-terms.mjs.

Review cases

Drafted communities without an answer key, for people to rate (RECOMMENDATIONS.md, part C). Set A comes with the values the community chose: rate the suggested approaches. Set B has no values: rate the suggested values. For each suggestion, is it fine or wrong for this community? Then the case as a whole, and a note on anything missing. Your ratings are kept in this browser as you go; download them to hand them in. File: data/review_cases.json.
