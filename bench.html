Recommendations workbench

For the team, to delete before launch: what's being reviewed at each stage, the benchmark communities, and a place to try the suggestions. Nothing here is saved, and nothing here changes the editor.

What to review, and when

The suggestions are being rebuilt (see RECOMMENDATIONS.md, “How we got here”). Each stage has its own files to review; the counts update from the files themselves.

Benchmark communities

Example communities with what they'd choose: the test cases the suggestions are measured against. They are not the type fit tables (those mark every option for every community type); they're what those tables have to get right. Review: does each community ring true, and are its choices right? good should be Suggested, also is fine as See also (or interesting for this community type), avoid should get no chip. File: data/benchmark_communities.json.

Tuning

How well the suggestions do, and with which settings. The benchmarks (18) are what the settings are tuned on; the held-out communities (10, drafted by web Claude, data/benchmark_heldout.json) are never tuned on, only checked, so they show whether a setting really works or just fits the 18 examples. In each cell: Suggested of the good choices (+ See also). The goal, in order: no to-avoid option gets a chip; short lists; as many good choices Suggested as possible. The search is node scripts/tune-recs.mjs (data/tuning_results.json); the editor's settings are TUNING in js/recommend.js, changed only by hand. Method and decisions: RECOMMENDATIONS.md, “Tuning”.

Type fit

For each community type (the kind of group), how typical each value is, and how well each approach suits it: marks from −2 (strongly against) to +2 (strongly for), each with a reason; a blank means no opinion. gate = generally not a fit (no chip, the reason shown); interesting = rare for this kind of group but viable. Review: are the marks and reasons right, and is anything important missing? Each type's benchmark communities are checked against its marks. Typical is the option's usual mark across all kinds of group (the median); suggestions will use the difference from it, so a mark only counts where this kind of group differs from groups in general. When a type looks right, Approve this type copies a command that marks it and its marks approved (scripts/review.mjs). Files: data/community_types.json, data/type_value_fit.json, data/type_approach_fit.json.

Across all community types

Rarely a fit anywhere

The same for every kind of group (not the type chosen above): options that are a poor fit almost everywhere get no chip, with the reason shown, unless a community type marks them for or interesting (data/option_flags.json). They replace gates that were repeated in most types.

Positions on the scales

Where each option and value sits on Participatory, Transparent and Hierarchical, from 1 to 7 (data/governance_scales.json). These positions give every published card its scales, and feed the scales signal of every suggestion. Review: check each option's three positions and its reason (the reason may be quoted to communities, so it should read well on its own). — means it says nothing about that scale. In the tables, P, T and H are the three scales. How each scale applies to decision-making, conflict, joining and values is under its definition below.

How a card's scores work

  • A card's score on each scale is the average of the scores of what it chose: its decision-making approaches, how people join (tiers and ways) and its conflict management approaches, rounded to a whole number. The card says what it's based on (e.g. “Participatory 5 / 7 · based on 6 choices”).
  • — means an option says nothing about that scale; it's left out of the average (not counted as low).
  • If nothing a card chose is scored on a scale, that scale shows N/A.
  • Only accepted scores count; proposed ones are drafts waiting for review.
  • A community also says where it would like to be on each scale (Basics, 1–7: its target scales, shown publicly only in Full). Suggestions compare these targets with the positions here: the scales signal (see RECOMMENDATIONS.md, “How we got here”).

Format × values

What the kind of online space (Basics' format: chat group, forum, social network, team…) says about values, only where it says something: the format signal for value suggestions (data/format_value_fit.json). The same whatever the community type.

Format × approaches

What the kind of online space says about approaches, whatever the community type: e.g. a team is a set group, not open to join, whatever kind of group it is (data/format_approach_fit.json, drafted after the held-out check and accepted 2026-10-10). The format signal for approach suggestions (weight 0.5, a specific signal). It took good approaches Suggested from 50 to 56 of 110 on the benchmarks and from 12 to 17 of 65 on the held-out set, with no to-avoid chips on either (RECOMMENDATIONS.md, “Tuning”).

Size fit

How well each option suits a community's size (data/approach_size_fit.json), from −2 (strains badly) to +2 (suits it well); — means size makes no real difference. Sizes: Small (under ~50), Medium (~50 to a few hundred), Large (a few hundred to a few thousand), XL very large (more).

The rule: score how the approach behaves at each size as it's normally used, without assuming more people take part in each process as the community grows. Strain counts only when the approach by its nature needs the whole membership (everyone must agree; every member approves each newcomer; a community-wide vote on a dispute), needs people to know each other personally, or makes a few people's work grow with every member or case (one leader deciding everything; a few reviewers reading every application). “Suits it” means designed for many people (delegation, sampling, standing bodies). Formal structure in a small group is overhead, not failure: at most −1. Rows re-scored with this rule show what they were.

Value groups and word lists

Each value has a word list: words and phrases that, when a community uses them in its Purpose, Culture or keywords, suggest that value. E.g. if Community care's list has “look after each other” and a family writes “we look after each other”, they see Community care suggested, with Because you wrote “look after each other”. Matching is by word stem, so “helping each other” also matches “help each other”; a trailing * matches any ending.

How these word lists were made

First drafts were written by hand. A script (scripts/make-value-terms.mjs) then tried mining CommunityRule's value descriptions for distinctive words and phrases (TF-IDF, by word stem). That gave mostly noise, because the descriptions are written as examples (“wheelchair ramps”), not the way communities talk; on the example communities it found fewer core values with twice the wrong ones, so it was set aside. The proposals are now each value's name and everyday phrases drafted by Claude (data/value_phrases_drafted.json); the script flags questionable words (common across many values) and misfires, and tests every list with the editor's own matching. Full account: DESIGN.md, “How the word lists were made”.

Proposed changes to the word lists

One row per value, with its whole word list as proposed: today's words, plus the new ones (the value's name, and everyday phrases drafted by Claude). Questionable: today's words that would be dropped, being so common they'd match too often. Misfires: words that suggested this value for an example community that wouldn't choose it (possibly noise, for you to judge). The lists in use now are in the table further down. Accept copies the command that applies a row (its list, minus questionable, plus new); for anything in between, tell Claude.
